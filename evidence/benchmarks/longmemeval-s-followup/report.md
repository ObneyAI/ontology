# Follow-up searches and quote boundaries · November 25, 2026

The incumbent is the 80-candidate ontology adapter with cross-encoder reranking and chronological evidence. We tested three ways to improve it at the same final 1,200-token evidence limit. Each experiment used full LongMemEval-S histories, a seeded local Gemma reader and paired comparisons on the same questions. The three new samples are disjoint by base question ID, question text and labeled supporting-session ID. The first two experiments used an additional planner call in the search arm. Some runs overlapped locally, so their wall times are diagnostic.

**Follow-up retrieval.** On the first 21 questions, the incumbent, a one-query planner and a revised reader instruction each scored 13/21 using the upstream task prompts with our local judge. All three abstained correctly on 3/3 unsupported questions. The original pilot grader gave 15/21, 15/21 and 14/21 respectively. The planner searched ten times and sometimes echoed its instruction. Requiring a structured plan and a source-grounded query addressed that failure mode. On 14 separate confirmation questions, the incumbent, repaired planner and reader-only alternative each scored 11/14 under the task prompts and 12/14 under the pilot grader. The repaired planner accepted three searches, rejected three plans and judged eight contexts sufficient. Both experiments produced identical per-question verdicts across arms under the task prompts. The planners consumed 32,795 and 22,273 additional input/output tokens across their respective sets.

**Where the evidence went.** Across those 35 questions the incumbent's 80 candidates included 52 of 53 annotated turns. The final contexts included 47. Restricting passage selection to the annotated turns after scoring recovered zero of eleven shared misses. This diagnostic still selects a bounded window per turn. A turn hit can therefore hide an omitted name or list entry. In a separate calibration check on two earlier examples, the task-specific grading prompts rejected an unsupported dinner-preference abstention and accepted the reference character name. Both grades reversed the generic judge.

**List quotations.** On two diagnostic questions, the graph had retrieved the correct assistant message and the quote had omitted the answer. One window started after the brand name in a numbered fashion list. A second window showed the opening of a 100-item list instead of item 27. The quote repair uses the nearest list-item label when a window starts inside an item, or a uniquely numbered entry when the question requests an explicit ordinal. It preserves the selected source IDs, reader instruction, model and token cap. Both graders credited “Veja” and “Sound effects” after the repair. These two cases were used to develop the change and are reported separately from the fresh sample.

On the third fresh 14-question sample, the repair changed quotations on eleven questions. Both configurations scored **9/14** with the task-specific rubric and **10/14** with the generic grader. No per-question rubric verdict changed, and both correctly abstained twice. A boundary check during this run found that a window already starting on an item label could shift to the preceding item. After tightening label and ordinal matching, two contexts changed. We reran those cases and verified the final offsets and lengths across all 14 questions. This check and the small sample limit the generalization claim. The demonstrated gain is confined to the two diagnosed clipping failures.

All runs used the local judge model. “Task-specific rubric” refers to the upstream prompts, not a score from an official hosted judge. The optional `ontology-quotes` arm is the targeted repair. The costly planner variants remain experimental.
